### 1. Mathematical Results via AI

* **Resolution of the Erdős–Graham Problem via the Erdős–Sós Conjecture in Dense Graphs**  
  [The Erd\H os-Sós conjecture in dense graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05417v1) — Bruce Reed, Maya Stein  
  **Mathematical Impact:** (Score: 5 — Landmark Breakthrough). The authors establish that for every $\gamma > 0$, there exists $n_0$ such that for all $n \ge n_0$ and $k \ge \gamma n$, any $n$-vertex graph with more than $(k-2)n/2$ edges contains every $k$-vertex tree. As a principal corollary, this result conclusively resolves a 51-year-old open conjecture of Erdős and Graham regarding the multicolor Ramsey numbers of trees. A companion paper, [The extremal cases of the Erd\H os--Sós conjecture](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05411v1), completes the resolution for graphs possessing high minimum degree subgraphs $H$ with $\delta(H) \ge (1-\mu)k$.

* **Proof of the Kontorovich–Nakamura Conjecture on Arithmetic Polyhedra**  
  [Arithmetic Polyhedra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05349v1) — Daniel Allcock, Pat Devlin, Anna Felikson, Alex Kontorovich, Ian Whitehead  
  **Mathematical Impact:** (Score: 5 — Landmark Breakthrough). Applying the Koebe–Andreev–Thurston theorem relating 3D hyperbolic reflection groups to combinatorial polyhedra, the authors settle the 2016 Kontorovich–Nakamura conjecture. They prove that all arithmetic ideal, right-angled hyperbolic polyhedra are commensurable to those formed by gluing together copies of three explicit "seed" polyhedra: the regular ideal tetrahedron, the square pyramid, and the cuboctahedron.

* **Log-Free Bound for Chowla's Cosine Problem**  
  [A Log-Free $n^{1/5}$ Bound for Chowla's Cosine Problem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05338v1) — Abhishek Shankar  
  **Mathematical Impact:** (Score: 4 — Substantive Mathematical Contribution). For a finite set of positive integers $S$, Chowla's cosine problem asks for bounds on $K(S) = -\min_{x \in \mathbb{T}} \sum_{s \in S} \cos(2\pi s x)$. Removing the logarithmic loss from Bedert's $K(S) \ge |S|^{1/5 - o(1)}$ bound, the author establishes the tight uniform lower bound $K(S) \ge c|S|^{1/5}$ for an absolute constant $c > 0$. The proof replaces multiplicative amplification with an exact averaging identity for asymmetric boundaries of additive intersections.

* **Verification of the MDS Conjecture for Two-Transitive Automorphism Groups**  
  [On the lengths of MDS codes with a two-transitive permutation automorphism group](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05292v1) — Haihua Deng, Tao Feng, Andrey V. Vasil'ev  
  **Mathematical Impact:** (Score: 4 — Substantive Mathematical Contribution). The authors prove that any $[n,k]_q$ Maximum Distance Separable (MDS) code with $4 \le k \le q-3$ that admits a 2-transitive permutation automorphism group satisfies $n \le q+1$. This establishes the classical MDS conjecture for all codes within this significant structural symmetry class.

### 2. Theorem Proving & Reasoning Architectures

* **AxQM: A Textbook-Scale Lean 4 Benchmark for Quantum Mechanics**  
  [AxQM: A Textbook-Scale Benchmark for Formal Proof Synthesis in a Library of Finite-Dimensional Quantum Mechanics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05157v1) — Weichen Winston Yin, Jacob M. Taylor, Dirk R. Englund, Frank H. L. Koppens  
  **Architectural Advance:** (Score: 4 — Substantive Formalization & Benchmark Architecture). AxQM introduces a formal physics proof-synthesis benchmark comprising 1,019 kernel-checkable tasks extracted from 479 items in Nielsen & Chuang’s *Quantum Computation and Quantum Information*. Built upon a custom Lean 4 library for finite-dimensional quantum mechanics, it expands physics formalization benchmarks fourfold. Every task features deterministically verifiable solutions verified via the Lean 4 kernel without unverified axioms or `sorry` stubs.

* **Automata-Theoretic Obstruction to Area-Dinv $q,t$-Catalan Symmetry**  
  [A Computational Obstruction to Swapping Area and Dinv: An Automata-Theoretic View of the $q,t$-Catalan Symmetry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05005v1) — Jineon Baek, Byung-Hak Hwang, Joonhyun La, Hongseok Yang  
  **Architectural / Theoretical Advance:** (Score: 4 — Substantive Theoretical Synthesis). Addressing the long-standing open problem in algebraic combinatorics of finding an explicit, length-preserving bijection that exchanges the `area` and `dinv` statistics on Dyck paths, the authors analyze the transformation mechanisms using automata theory. They define *weighted-rank polyregular maps* (WRP) and prove that no WRP map can swap `area` and `dinv`, formally establishing a computational boundary that explains why classical rank-sorting strategies cannot yield explicit bijective proofs.

* **RISE: Self-Extrapolating Policy Distillation for Reasoning LLMs**  
  [RISE: Recursive Improvement via Self-Extrapolating Policy Distillation](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.05295) — Yang Li, Semih Yavuz, Shafiq Joty  
  **Reasoning Architecture:** (Score: 3 — Notable Methodology). RISE addresses the teacher quality bottleneck in on-policy distillation by generating synthetic dense targets directly from a model's RLVR training trajectory. By extrapolating parameter and logit displacements between current checkpoints and trailing anchors, RISE converts sparse outcome rewards into dense token-level target distributions without external teachers or privileged context, achieving sustained recursive improvements across complex mathematical reasoning benchmarks.
